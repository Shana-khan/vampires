From Blood to Vital Energy

A Study of the Rituals of the Order of the Red Dragon
and the Brotherhood of the Black Waters

- Karolina Wiśniewski -

The Order of the Red Dragon and the Brotherhood of the Black Waters are two secret societies whose history remains largely shrouded in mystery, rumor, and legend. While the Brotherhood of the Black Waters is clearly linked to its founder, Irina Stanescu-Bibescu, the Order of the Red Dragon is frequently — and wrongly — attributed to Dorian Bibescu. This confusion stems more from popular association than from verified facts.

Both organizations have been associated with blood rituals, giving rise to criminal myths vigorously promoted by their detractors. The purpose of this study is to place these practices back in their historical, archaeological, and symbolic context, clearly distinguishing the energetic and spiritual use of blood from accusations of criminality, while examining the Thracian-Dacian heritage and modern reinterpretations.

1. Historical Context and Origins

The Order of the Red Dragon appears in reliable sources at the beginning of the 19th century. The oldest known document, dated September 20, 1807, mentions Andrei Odobescu, described as a Rosicrucian and Grand Master of the Order. This period corresponds to a vast spiritual revival across Europe, marked by the rise of Hermetic societies, Masonic lodges, Theosophy, and occult movements inspired by ancient traditions.

The writings of Papus (Gérard Encausse) and Éliphas Lévi, at the end of the century, reflect the desire to restore to occult rites and the symbolism of blood a spiritual and energetic dimension. The practices of these societies are based on the circulation of vital energy, meditation, and initiation, with the aim of inner transformation of the adept.

Contrary to popular narratives, no credible evidence links the Order of the Red Dragon to the Order of the Dragon founded in 1408 by Sigismund of Luxembourg. The connection is based primarily on the similarity of names. The Brotherhood of the Black Waters, by contrast, benefits from more precise documentation, particularly regarding its blood rituals inspired by ancient traditions and incorporating elements of feminine magic.

2. Criminal Accusations and the Construction of Myths

The criminal reputation of these societies rests mainly on exaggerations and rumors. From the 19th century onward, anti-esoteric pamphlets and sensationalist newspapers spoke of human sacrifices and bloody practices, sometimes including the ingestion of blood. These accusations, similar to those made by the Inquisition or against certain Masonic lodges, often served to justify repression, imprisonment, and social ostracism.

Available internal archives present a very different picture. The rituals were limited to voluntary offerings of blood, regarded as vectors of vital energy and means of communication with spiritual entities. Blood served as an energetic medium to enhance concentration, meditative states, and the effectiveness of initiation ceremonies. The goal was neither violence nor crime, but spiritual transformation and the transmission of vital energy.

3. Thracian-Dacian Heritage and the Central Role of Blood

These societies draw heavily from the cults of the Thracian-Dacian peoples, the Getae, and the Triballi. Blood occupied a central place in rites, oaths, and funerary ceremonies. Herodotus reports that every five years the Getae sacrificed a man chosen by lot to send him as a messenger to Zalmoxis.

Warrior alliances also involved blood rituals: participants would prick their arm or tongue and mix a few drops of blood with wine or mead, consumed collectively to seal a sacred pact. These practices are confirmed by archaeological finds in the Orăștie Mountains. The ritual suicides during the fall of Sarmizegetusa in 106 CE further illustrate blood’s role as a sacred link between ruler and community.

4. Menstrual Blood and Feminine Magic

The Brotherhood of the Black Waters also incorporates practices centered on menstrual blood, regarded as a powerful vector of subtle energy. Archaeology confirms these uses: ceramic and bronze cups containing traces of human hematin have been discovered in exclusively female pits in the sanctuaries of the Orăștie Mountains. Considered impure in daily life, this blood became hyper-sacred when handled by initiated women, priestesses, or sorceresses, highlighting the central importance of feminine magic within the Brotherhood.

5. Symbolism and Contemporary Practices

In both the Order of the Red Dragon and the Brotherhood of the Black Waters, blood was never used for criminal purposes, but as a symbolic and energetic medium. Adepts believe it contains a subtle essence capable of circulating through the chakras and nourishing vital energy.

The esoteric writings of Papus and Éliphas Lévi emphasize its role as an energetic catalyst in rituals.

6. Symbolic and Energetic Interpretation of Blood

Whether menstrual or voluntarily offered, blood constitutes an essential vector of life force and a mediator between the human and the divine. In the Thracian-Dacian heritage, it symbolizes communication with Zalmoxis and the strengthening of alliances. In modern societies, it becomes a tool of inner magic.

Conclusion

The study of the Order of the Red Dragon and the Brotherhood of the Black Waters reveals a complex interplay between ancient heritage, symbolic practices, and social myths. Blood plays a central role — not as a criminal instrument, but as a conduit of vital energy and spiritual connection. When placed in their proper historical, archaeological, and symbolic context, these practices reclaim their initiatory and energetic dimension.
